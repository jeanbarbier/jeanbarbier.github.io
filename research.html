Deep networks beyond the linear regime

Most exact theories of neural networks either linearise them or take a width so large that features barely move. We study networks trained on a number of samples comparable to their number of parameters. There we characterise when a network is information-theoretically equivalent to a linear model, and when it instead learns features through sharp specialisation transitions, with Bayes-optimal error curves and the scaling laws that follow from them.

Matrix inference and random matrices

Many learning problems reduce to recovering a large matrix from noisy observations: denoising, factorisation, PCA with structured noise, estimation in time series. We compute their information-theoretic limits, including the hard regimes where the rank grows with the dimension or rotational invariance is lost, and design message-passing algorithms that reach them.

Training, fine-tuning and representations

Optimal-performance results say what is possible, not what training finds. We also study what gradient-based training does: when pre-training helps or hurts low-rank fine-tuning, and how noise in the labels shapes the information stored in learned representations.

Rigorous foundations

Much of the statistical physics we use rests on non-rigorous methods. A long-standing line of our work turns replica predictions into theorems: interpolation and adaptive interpolation methods, concentration of overlaps, and the optimality of approximate message passing, for estimation, generalised linear models and coding.
